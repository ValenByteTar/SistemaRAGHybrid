CISSP Certification — Eight Domains of Knowledge

The Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) is an advanced
certification offered by (ISC)2 (now ISC2). It is designed for experienced security
practitioners, managers, and executives who design, implement, and manage a best-in-class
cybersecurity program. The certification requires at least five years of cumulative
paid work experience in two or more of the eight domains of the CISSP Common Body
of Knowledge (CBK).

The CISSP exam covers eight domains:

Domain 1: Security and Risk Management (16% of exam)
Covers confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA triad), risk management
processes, governance, compliance, legal and regulatory issues, business continuity
planning, and security policies and procedures.

Domain 2: Asset Security (10% of exam)
Addresses data classification, data ownership, privacy, asset handling, and data
remanence. Includes data lifecycle management and data security controls.

Domain 3: Security Architecture and Engineering (13% of exam)
Covers security models, system architecture, security capabilities of information
systems, cryptography, and physical security design principles.

Domain 4: Communication and Network Security (13% of exam)
Addresses network architecture, secure network components, secure communication
channels, and network attacks and countermeasures. Includes TCP/IP, firewalls,
VPN, and network access control.

Domain 5: Identity and Access Management (IAM) (13% of exam)
Covers authentication, authorization, and accountability. Includes access control
models (MAC, DAC, RBAC), identity management lifecycle, and credential management
practices such as single sign-on (SSO) and multi-factor authentication (MFA).

Domain 6: Security Assessment and Testing (12% of exam)
Addresses vulnerability assessments, penetration testing, security audits, log
management, and continuous monitoring. Includes assessment types (internal, external,
white-box, black-box, grey-box).

Domain 7: Security Operations (13% of exam)
Covers incident response, disaster recovery, access control implementation, security
operations center (SOC) activities, and day-to-day security operations.

Domain 8: Software Development Security (10% of exam)
Addresses security in the software development lifecycle (SDLC), secure coding
practices, malware types, and database security. Includes OWASP Top 10 and
DevSecOps practices.

The exam consists of 100-150 questions in a Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT)
format for English-language candidates, with a time limit of 3 hours. The passing
score is 700 out of 1000 points.
